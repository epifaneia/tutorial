AI training for your staff, free and built around roles

‘A Journey Through AI’ is a free online course that teaches people to use AI at work, and to do it without putting data at risk, with a different route for each role. For a company, it gives everyone a shared grounding in about an hour and leaves a training record that each person can hand in when they finish.

Art. 4

The EU AI Act requires companies that use AI to ‘take measures to support the development of AI literacy of their staff’. National authorities have supervised it since 2 August 2026.

Sources: Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 and the European Commission.

What the law asks

Article 4, as written

Article 4 of the EU AI Act was amended in July 2026. This is paragraph 1 as it now stands:

‘Providers and deployers of AI systems shall take measures to support the development of AI literacy of their staff and other persons dealing with the operation and use of AI systems on their behalf, taking into account their technical knowledge, experience, education and training and the context the AI systems are to be used in, and considering the persons or groups of persons on whom the AI systems are to be used. This obligation does not require providers or deployers to guarantee any specific level of AI literacy of any individual.’

Article 4(1) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, as replaced by Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 (Official Journal of the European Union, 24 July 2026).

In plain terms: if your company uses an AI system in its work, it is a ‘deployer’ and has to do something to help its people understand the AI they work with. What it does should fit each job and each use. The law doesn't prescribe a course, a number of hours or a test.

This course can be one of the measures your company takes on AI literacy. It does not prove compliance on its own, and no course does.

A separate point, for companies with staff in Spain: if you use AI to make decisions about people (shifts, hiring, appraisals), you have information duties of your own, towards workers' representatives (Article 64.4.d of the Workers' Statute, the Estatuto de los Trabajadores) and towards each worker (Royal Decree 723/2026, in force since 5 October 2026; in Spanish). The course covers them in the Management and IT journeys.

How to use it in your company

Four steps

  1. Decide which role each person takes

    There are five: Employee, Management, Data analysis, Marketing and IT. If in doubt, choose Employee: the basics are there and they apply to any job. The table below says who each one is for.

  2. Set aside an hour, or two sittings

    An hour of working time is enough for Employee, Data analysis and Marketing. Management and IT need about two hours. It can be split into two sessions: progress is saved in the browser, so people need to come back on the same device and the same browser.

  3. Ask for the training record

    On finishing, each person can print, or save as a PDF, a training record with their name, their role, each stop passed with its date, and the content version. Ask for it and file it with your other training records. It is the only trace there is: the course doesn't notify anyone.

  4. Add what the course can't know

    The course explains the general framework. Your policy, your tools and your contacts have to come from you. The details are further down.

Itinerary by role

Who does what, and how long it takes

The journey has five stops: the chat, the single-task tool, AI connected to company data, agents, and the custom-built tool. Each stop has two paths, ‘Get the most out of it’ and ‘Do it safely’. Management and IT have a third, ‘Roll it out’. Each path takes about seven minutes: short answers, a worked example told as a comic strip, three key ideas, links to official sources and a five-question quiz. To pass, you need all five right.

What you need to add

Three things only your company knows

  • Your AI usage policy. What is allowed, what for, and which data never goes into an AI tool. If you don't have one yet, a single page is enough to start with.
  • Your approved tools. The course explains each kind of tool. Which one is approved in your company, and which account people sign in with, is for you to say.
  • Who to tell when something goes wrong. A name or a mailbox: your data protection officer, IT or your incident channel. The course teaches people to speak up early; what's missing is who to.

You can hand all this out on one sheet with the invitation. The organiser's kit (in Spanish) has an invitation email, talking points for a kick-off meeting and a record sheet.

What the course is not

Better to know beforehand

  • It isn't official training, and it isn't set up for subsidised-training schemes. It doesn't award a qualification. The certificate at the end is for information only, and the training record is generated in each person's browser: a record, not a verifiable certificate.
  • It doesn't track who finishes. I don't know who starts it or who completes it, and neither does your company, except through the training record each person gives you. There is no package for your learning platform either.
  • It doesn't replace your data protection officer or your legal advisers. It explains the general framework in Spain and Europe as at its review date. It doesn't know your case.

Privacy

Nothing leaves each person's browser

The course uses no cookies, asks for no sign-up and loads nothing from third parties: no analytics and no external fonts. Progress, and the name each person types for their certificate, are stored only in their own browser. They don't reach me or anyone else.

So there are no accounts to create and no staff data to hand over in order to use it.
